Which values go in the table?

Enter blank-corrected rates already converted with your optical calibration, at one enzyme concentration (or divided by enzyme concentration). Keep near-zero and slightly negative rates; dropping them biases the fit.

Use the pH measured in the final reaction mixture. Name the buffer on every row so overlaps can be compared.

Fit results

Standard errors are approximate (from the curvature of the fit at its best values), assuming independent reactions with equal scatter. They do not include uncertainty in blanks, calibration, or pH measurement.

Model comparison

AICc balances fit quality against the number of parameters; lower is better. A difference under about 2 means the data do not clearly favor one model.

Buffer overlaps

Mean rate at each pH measured in two buffers. If the buffers disagree beyond their scatter, part of the apparent pH dependence may be a buffer effect, and a single curve through both is not justified.

Recovery after exposure

Rate in the standard assay after exposure at each pH

A low rate in the profile with normal recovery points to an effect of the assay pH on catalysis. Activity that stays low after return points to damage during exposure.

Residuals

Measured − fitted rate

Scatter around zero is consistent with the model. A run of points on one side, or a step between buffers, suggests the model or the buffers are the problem.

What is being fit?

Two ionizations

y = ymax / (1 + 10pKa1 − pH + 10pH − pKa2)

One ionizable group must be deprotonated (pKa1) and another protonated (pKa2) for activity. ymax is a fitted scale. The curve peaks at pH = (pKa1 + pKa2)/2, and the peak is below ymax, by a lot when the two pKa values are close.

Single limb

rising: y = ymax / (1 + 10pKa1 − pH)
falling: y = ymax / (1 + 10pH − pKa2)

Use these when the measured range covers only one side of the profile.

All models are fitted by least squares on the original rate scale, using every included reaction with equal weight. These are apparent pKa values for the whole system at your substrate and cofactor concentrations; the fit does not identify a residue.
