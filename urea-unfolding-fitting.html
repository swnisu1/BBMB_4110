Urea Unfolding Fitting

Load the emission spectra from the plate reader (Tecan .xlsx as exported). The matching no-protein urea blank is subtracted from every spectrum. A chosen intensity channel is fit to a two-state model with sloping baselines to give Cm, m and ΔGwater; F350/F330 is shown as a separate view of the transition. Everything runs in your browser. 2Channels

The ratio is Fb/Fa (F350/F330 by default). Tryptophan emission shifts to longer wavelength as it becomes exposed to water, so the ratio rises on unfolding. ± 0 uses the single nearest reading (interpolated if needed).

3Fit

“Both together” shares Cm and m between the two channels and gives each channel its own baselines. Each channel is weighted by 1/(its scatter in a separate fit) so neither dominates.

Each well shows its spectrum (blank subtracted for protein wells), the ratio Fb/Fa and the urea concentration. The coloured bar is the protein. Yellow: precipitate recorded (P). Well

Plate layout: what is in each well?

Type the final urea concentration (M) in each well. For each row, enter the protein (WT, mutant…). Rows with the same protein in the same read are replicates and are fit together. Set a row to blank for the no-protein urea series; give the blank wells their urea too, so each protein well is matched to the blank at the same urea. Leave the urea empty for wells that should not be used. A reread of the same plate starts with the same layout.

Spectra

Mean spectrum at each urea concentration (replicates averaged), blank subtracted. Normalised scales each spectrum to its own maximum, which shows the shift in the emission maximum.

Fitted series

Click a row to look at that series in detail. ΔGwater = m·Cm; its SE includes the correlation between m and Cm. For oligomeric Lk-ADH these are apparent two-state parameters.

Intensity vs. urea

Residuals (data − fit)

Fraction unfolded

Each point is converted with its own series' fitted baselines: fU = (F − FN) / (FU − FN), where FN = aN + bND and FU = aU + bUD. This puts WT and mutant on one scale; the dotted lines mark each Cm.

Ratio vs. urea

The ratio is shown as a separate display of the transition and is not fit. A ratio is not proportional to the fraction unfolded when the two states differ in total brightness, so its midpoint can differ from the fitted Cm.

Repeat reads: still changing?

Each protein fit separately in each read (same channels and options). If Cm or the ratio still moves between reads by more than the uncertainty, the samples were not yet at equilibrium; use the later read, or incubate longer.
