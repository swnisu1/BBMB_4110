Load the absorbance time courses from the plate reader (the Tecan .xlsx as exported). Look at each well and pick the straight early part for the slope. Enter the [S] in each well and the enzyme concentration, then convert ΔA340/min to µM NADPH/min. The rates are fit to Michaelis–Menten, Hill and substrate-inhibition models. 3ΔA340 → µM NADPH

Concentration = A / (ε·l). In a plate the light passes vertically, so the path length l is the liquid depth: volume ÷ well area. Meniscus shape makes this approximate. A plate factor from an NADPH standard curve in the same plate and volume is better.

4Enzyme and fit

[E] is the final concentration in the well; it gives kcat = Vmax/[E]. Series names are set by row in the plate layout.

Each well shows its trace (whole run; shaded part = slope window), the slope in mA340/min, and its [S] once entered. ! marks a trace that bends inside the window, a poor straight-line fit, or absorbance near the detector limit.

Set this well is to left out only for a well that went wrong (bubble, pipetting error, empty); it then drops out of the rates and the fit. Drag across the plot to set the slope window, or drag an edge of the shaded band to move it. The window applies to all wells unless “own window” is ticked, in which case it applies to this well only. Click a point to drop it from the slope (for a bubble or mixing spike); click it again to restore it.

Plate layout: what is in each well?

Leave [S] empty for wells that should not be fit. Wells with the same series name and [S] are replicates; series let you compare WT and a mutant on one plate. You can paste a row or block of numbers copied from Excel into the boxes. Blank wells (no enzyme) only set the background slope and need no [S].

Choose the model to show above or by clicking a row. BIC = n ln(RSS/n) + (k + 1) ln(n); lower is better. The weight is roughly the probability of each model; a ΔBIC above 10 is very strong evidence.
