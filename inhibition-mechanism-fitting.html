Inhibition Mechanism Fitting

Fit initial-velocity data collected at several inhibitor concentrations to competitive, uncompetitive, noncompetitive and mixed inhibition, and let BIC say which model the data support. Assumes the cofactor is held at a saturating concentration.

Slopes

Use the early part of each trace, where it is still straight. This is the shared window for every well. To change one well only, use its sliders or drag across its plot on the right.

ΔA340 → µM NADPH

Concentration = A / (ε·l). In a plate the light passes vertically, so the path length l is the liquid depth: volume ÷ well area. Meniscus shape makes this approximate. A plate factor from an NADPH standard curve in the same plate and volume is better.

Each well shows its trace (whole run; shaded part = slope window), the slope in mA340/min, and its [S] once entered. Colour shows how well a straight line fits the window:goodworth a look (slope changes 20–45% across the window, or R² 0.95–0.98)problem (strong bend, R² below 0.95, A above 2, or too few points). Hover over a well, or click it, to see why. A no-enzyme blank has substrate, NADP⁺ and buffer but no enzyme; the mean slope of the blank wells is subtracted from every sample.

Plate layout: what is in each well?

Type the substrate concentration in each well, as it is on the plate, the inhibitor concentration [I] for each row (0 for no inhibitor), and the final enzyme concentration [E] in the well (used for v/[E] and kcat). [I] uses the same units as [S]. If every row has the same [S] series, fill in the top row and press Copy the top row's [S] to every row. You can also paste a row or block of numbers copied from Excel. Rows with the same [I] are replicates and are fit together. Set a row to blank for the no-enzyme background; it needs no [S]. Leave [S] empty for wells that should not be fit. Fill the class plate map enters 2, 5, 10, 20, 40, 80 mM in columns 1–6 and 7–12, and [I] = 0, 1.5, 3, 9 mM in rows A–D.

Model comparison

Click a row to show that model in the plots. BIC = n ln(SSE/n) + k ln(n); lower is better. The weight is the approximate probability of each model among those compared (Δ BIC above 10 is very strong evidence).
