Use the early part of each trace, where it is still straight. This is the shared window for every well. To change one well only, drag across its plot on the right.

3ΔA340 → µM NADPH

Concentration = A / (ε·l). In a plate the light passes vertically, so the path length l is the liquid depth: volume ÷ well area. Meniscus shape makes this approximate. A plate factor from an NADPH standard curve in the same plate and volume is better.

Each well shows its trace (whole run; shaded part = slope window), the slope in mA340/min, and its [S] once entered. A no-enzyme blank is a well with substrate, NADP⁺ and buffer but no enzyme; the mean slope of these wells is subtracted from every sample as background. A well with [S] = 0 is not a blank; leave it as a sample, since it is a point on the curve.

Well

Plate layout: what is in each well?

Grid shows

Use Grid shows to pick what you are typing into the wells: the varied substrate [S]; the second substrate [S₂] (for example NADP⁺, needed only for two-substrate experiments); the inhibitor [I] (0 for no inhibitor; only for inhibition experiments); the enzyme (WT, mutantA…); and its final [E] in the well. Leave [S₂] and [I] empty if you did not vary them. Every well has its own values, so the plate can be laid out any way you like. Wells with [E] = 0 are no-enzyme blanks: their mean slope is the background, and they need no [S].
