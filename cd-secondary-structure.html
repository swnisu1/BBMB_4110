CD Secondary Structure

Load a buffer spectrum and one or more protein spectra. The buffer is subtracted, the signal is converted to mean residue ellipticity, and the helix content is estimated. A file for the BeStSel server gives the full helix / sheet / turn / other breakdown. All tools

1 Spectra

Text or CSV exports from the CD instrument work as saved: a column of wavelengths (nm), a column of CD (mdeg) and, if the instrument writes it, a column of HT voltage (V). Header lines are skipped.

For each protein enter its concentration in the cuvette, its number of residues (including the His tag) and its molecular weight (needed for mg/mL). Our WT construct: 272 residues, 28,900 Da. A 1 mm cuvette is 0.1 cm.

3 Usable range

Below some wavelength the buffer and protein absorb so much light that the detector voltage (HT) climbs and the CD signal becomes noise. Points are used only where HT stays below the limit for both the protein and the buffer, and only above the shortest wavelength you set. Check your instrument's HT limit (often 600–700 V).

4 Save results

The BeStSel file has two columns, wavelength (nm) and Δε (M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ per residue), over the usable range up to 250 nm, one file per protein. At bestsel.elte.hu choose “Single spectrum”, paste or upload the file and set the units to Δε. BeStSel needs data down to at least 200 nm; 190 nm or lower gives a more reliable sheet estimate.

Grey: outside the usable range. An α-helix gives minima near 208 and 222 nm and a maximum near 192 nm; β-sheet gives a single minimum near 215–218 nm; a disordered chain gives a minimum near 198 nm.

Helix content from single wavelengths

Two classic estimates of the helix fraction, fH, from one wavelength each. Chen et al. (1974): fH = ([θ]222 + 2340) / −30300. Greenfield and Fasman (1969): fH = (−[θ]208 − 4000) / (33000 − 4000). Both use [θ] in deg cm² dmol⁻¹ and assume the rest of the protein contributes little at that wavelength, so they are approximate. Sheet and turn content need the whole spectrum: use the BeStSel file. Both estimates scale directly with the concentration you entered, so a concentration error moves them; the ratio [θ]222/[θ]208 does not depend on concentration.
